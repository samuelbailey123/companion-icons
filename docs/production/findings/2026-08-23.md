## Corrections to earlier notes

Two claims in the first pass were wrong. Both are recorded here because the advice they
produced would have made the picture **worse**, not better.

### The face was not underexposed

The first pass reported the speaker's face at 49 IRE, 47 IRE below his shirt, and
concluded "the key light is on his chest, not his face". That measurement used a fixed
sample box which landed on his **beard and mouth**, not lit skin.

Measured properly — forehead patch, then skin-tone detection across 13 frames spread
through the sermon:

| | Measured |
|---|---|
| Forehead, single frame | **74 IRE** |
| Lit skin, median across the sermon | **62 IRE** |
| Same, after the post grade | **69 IRE** |

62 IRE is slightly under the 65–70 target, not a fault. **The problem is the highlights,
not the exposure level** — the shirt still clips 79% flat at 235.

The corrective action inverts with the correction: enable the camera's **knee**, do
**not** stop down. Stopping down would have pushed faces genuinely dark.

**Lesson:** never measure a moving subject with a fixed box. Detect the subject, or verify
the box visually first. Skin detection that worked: chroma window U 95–127, V 138–172,
luma > 60, upper ¾ of frame only, then take the 75th percentile of luma across many
frames.

### Whole-frame median luma is not scene brightness

The first pass used "median luma 38–43 out of 235" as evidence of a very dark stage. That
figure is dominated by the near-black LED wall filling most of the frame and says almost
nothing about light falling on the subject. The stage lighting is in fact reasonably
bright — it had recently been upgraded.

## PTZ behaviour during worship

**Confirmed: it makes long moves live on air.** Between 04:00 and 25:00 there are only
about ten cuts in twenty-one minutes, with unbroken shots of 295 s, 266 s, 213 s and
203 s. The 14:13–18:39 shot is a continuous four-minute zoom from a close-up of one singer
out to the whole team, entirely on air.

### Ruled out, with evidence

Recorded so nobody chases them again.

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-iris pumping | **No** | Whole-frame luma swings 42→99 as the LED wall cycles, while the singers' skin holds at 35–52. If iris were chasing the wall, skin would move with it |
| Auto white balance drifting | **No** | Skin chroma holds at U 118–124 / V 143–153 across four minutes of the wall cycling pink→blue→purple. That is a locked WB |
| Frozen / dropped frames | **No** | 5.7% of frames looked near-duplicate, concentrated after minute 26. False alarm: during announcements the camera is locked, the speaker nearly still, and the image clean enough (noise sigma 0.68) that consecutive frames are legitimately near-identical. Real frame doubling **alternates** every other frame; this was uniform across all of them |

### Measurement techniques that do not work

- **Scene detection to find shots when a PTZ is zooming.** A zoom is not a cut, so
  `select='gt(scene,...)'` reported the four-minute zoom as one static shot. It also fires
  ~125×/minute on the pre-service countdown video.
- **Temporal per-pixel noise as a camera fingerprint.** It is dominated by camera
  *motion*, not sensor noise — a locked shot of a still speaker measured 0.00 sigma, which
  is impossible for a real sensor. Any camera comparison has to control for motion first.

### The vertical-clip trap

Worth its own note. Auto-reframing 16:9 → 9:16 by tracking the subject each frame
**visibly shakes**, because the luma centroid follows the speaker's *arms*. The first
attempt had one clip's crop travelling 2301 px inside a 432 px span.

What works: split at shot cuts, take the **median** position per shot, and decide whether
a shot needs to move at all from the 10th–90th percentile of subject position rather than
min/max. Most shots then lock outright, and a crop that never moves cannot shake. Only
genuinely traversing shots get a follow, sampled at 12.5/s so each step is ~5 px.

**For auto-reframe, stillness beats accuracy.** An imperfectly centred locked frame looks
professional; a perfectly centred moving one looks broken.
